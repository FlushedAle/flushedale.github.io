Recently I'm trying to listen to myself more. I don't do additional coding or 'hobby' that is work after work if I don't really feel like it. I feel like it silently leads to burnout. And with ADHD the effects are tripled.

I was especially annoyed by my progress in recent years and my capabiltiies of achieving the goals I've set to myself. Completely ignoring the fact that I am human, having attention issues(I avoid medication) and I don't even stop to ask myself whether I like what I'm doing. So I stopped for a bit. I tried to rearrange the way I approach learning stuff trying to get myself the best enviroment possible and stop trying to judge myself for walking away with thoughts or interests instead of doing something that I consider - I should, which first - I don't, it's my free time - second - I will walk away with thoughts anyway so start accepting that.

Bit by bit - I've repaired old laptop - traded for even older laptop and then proceeded to throw away my new windows based hardware (which i like but hell it distracts me so easily and I have both games and work on one pc then) and I've installed linux on an old aspire. (it loaded windows for 10 mins and couldn't handle chrome beforehand)

And honestly - this idea was kind of a game changer. I've done a few days trip of setuping stuff hoping I can fit every part of game development and programming on this machine. And mostly - I've succeeded. The only thing that was way too much annoyance was trying to get all my audio plugins to work so I stuck with workaround - I plan to create foley and audio design without windows plugins and music or everything that I need my windows plugins to achieve I'll do on a seperate windows machine.

In terms of my attention span - honestly - with my whole heart - it's great. 

I have my godot running in compatibility mode, have to put spotify on my phone instead of pc because it lags hell out of this hardware but the rewards are immense. I have joy from coding again aswell as am able to work several hours a day without continous ongoing frustration.

Since I made my linux laptop a much better isolated enviroment I started reading books too. Just like that. It is kind of playing to my advantage there. I am using pc by habit anyway, so why not make it perfect space to learn.

I've finished "The Foley Grail" since I wanted to know more techniques of recording sound effects for games and film but frankly - I was kind of dissapointed. I've bookmarked two places in a book which I'll visit again but I find it more of a history book with just some tiny bit of tips regarding props. I know I've read a different book before with more real life stuff but honestly - I couldn't find it back then.

Another book that I've started reading is The Art Of Game Design which I treat more like a workbook. I've heard before that the author writes full of himself etc. but I don't see that. And I am fast to reject authority so I don't know. What I find useful is being able to write down the set of questions being asked about my game from each set of "lens" (sort of point of view) and then I answer them in writing. This allows me to focus on those aspects and really think through my design without having to resort to someone telling me what to do.

Another big thing I've begun is Handmade Hero by Casey Muratori. This is a good one. I've gone through all basic C videos (solid 8 hours of information and practice). I am programming for nearly 10 years and yet I've learned so much it's incredible. And he hasn't even started a series by then! The first episode of Handmade Hero itself was also kind of revolutionary to me in a way. We've used nothing but Windows API to both setup, create and build game with microsoft's compiler(no builder bullshit which I really hate - why need to download 12 things to compile a language?).


I am moving at a steady pace and take breaks which I suggest you do too. Oh and one more important thing. Many people talk about this but this is not stressed enough.

If you're learning something, hell even when writing code as a hobby - DO NOT use Chat GPT and alikes. Seriously. I've done it over last years. And result is my knowledge started growing at much slower pace, I've started to use chat instead of my brain first (or even second, third it doesn't matter) and overally became a worse programmer, critical thinker, problem solver.

If you care about knowing how to program, want to make it a good paying job just learn to read documentations.
